Upper Kachura Lake

Foroq Tso (Lake) or Upper Kachura Lake is in the Karakoram mountain range of the western Himalayas, the greater Baltistan Region, and in the Indus River basin. The lake is of clear water and has a depth of 70 metres (230 ft). In summer it has a temperature of 15 °C (59 °F).[3] In winter the surface is frozen solid. The upper Indus River flows nearby at a lower elevation.

Soq Valley Kachura

Soq Valley is a picturesque, cup-shaped mountain valley located near Upper Kachura Lake in Skardu, Gilgit-Baltistan, Pakistan. Just past Shangrila Resort and Upper Kachura Lake (turning left at the fork past Upper Kachura), roughly 23–25 km from Skardu City (a 30 to 45-minute drive).

Shangrilla Lake

Shangrila Lake, is a lake located near the city of Skardu in Gilgit-Baltistan, Pakistan.[1] Situated at the height of 2,500 metres (8,200 ft), it has had an operational resort for tourists on its bank since 1983. Through the architectural work of the resort and the background Karakoram hills, the landscapes of the lake make it one of the attractive tourist destinations within northern Pakistan.

Kharpocho Fort

Kharpocho Fort is an ancient fort known as Kharpocho Fort (King of Forts) situated on a hill overlooking Skardu town. It was constructed during 1490-1505 by Ali Sher Khan Anchan, the Maqpoon dynasty of Skardu originated in early 3rd century AD after a long Tibetan rule.

Manthal Buddha Rock

Manthal Buddha Rock ('Dray chatfi Fong' in local Balti language) is a large granite rock with a relief sculpture of Buddha, which probably dates back to the 8th century. This rock is located in Manthal village in Skardu, in Pakistan. Buddha Rock is one of the most important relics of Buddhism in Skardu.

Satpara Lake

Satpara Lake, also spelled Sadpara (Urdu: سدپارہ جھیل) is a natural lake near Skardu in the Gilgit-Baltistan region of Pakistan. The lake supplies water to Skardu Valley through the Satpara Stream. It is fed by the melting glacial ice from the surrounding mountains,while the Satpara Stream is the lake's only outlet, which drains into the Indus River. Satpara Lake is situated at an elevation of 2,600 m (8,500 ft) above sea level.

Manthokha Waterfall

Manthokha Waterfall also spelt Manthoka Waterfall, is a waterfall located in the Kharmang Valley, Skardu, in Gilgit-Baltistan, in the extreme northern region of Pakistan. The waterfall has surface height of approximately180 feet (55 m), and located at a distance of around 80 kilometres (50 mi) from Skardu, the largest city of Gilgit Baltistan.

Saling is a scenic village and valley located in the Ghanche District of Gilgit-Baltistan, Pakistan, situated on the right bank of the Shyok River directly opposite Khaplu town. A popular picnic and trout-farming spot featuring natural spring-fed water pools surrounded by mountains.

Nansoq Village

Nansoq (also spelled Nangsoq) is Pakistan's first organic village, located just 3 kilometers behind the historic Kharpocho Fort in Skardu, Gilgit-Baltistan. Locals grow fruits, vegetables, and herbs using natural methods without synthetic fertilizers, pesticides, or heavy machinery. Houses are built using local stone, mud, and wood, preserving the authentic Balti style of living.
